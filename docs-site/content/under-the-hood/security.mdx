# Security model

## Key separation

Three distinct authorities, deliberately kept apart:

- **The voucher signer** — the only key the backend holds. Its worst-case
  abuse is issuing a voucher with a wrong *yield percentage*; it cannot touch
  principal.
- **The program upgrade authority** — kept cold, offline, and separate from
  the voucher signer. Program changes are visible on-chain before they affect
  anyone.
- **Your wallet** — the only key that can ever be the destination of your
  principal.

## Blast-radius limits

- **Beta caps**: $50 per lock, $1,000 platform TVL — enforced in-program, so
  the maximum at risk during beta is bounded regardless of any bug above the
  chain.
- **180-day force return**: the recovery path of last resort requires nothing
  from Locked In — not our servers, not our keys, not our existence.
- **Narrow program surface**: custody, settle, pot, force-return. No admin
  withdrawal instruction exists.

## Backend hardening

- Sessions require a wallet-signature challenge on top of Privy auth.
- Enrollment is confirmed by **reading the chain**, not by trusting the
  client's claim of a deposit.
- All money-adjacent writes are idempotent — retries and race conditions
  can't double-credit or double-judge.
- Mainnet deployments run boot-time guards that refuse to start with dev
  secrets, dev yield profiles, or misconfigured programs.
